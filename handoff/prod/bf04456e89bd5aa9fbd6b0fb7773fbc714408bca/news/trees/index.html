The Tree Update

The trees in New Haven are now their real size, there are 35 kinds of them, and every forest has been replanted. The terrain, your builds and your claims are where you left them, but the woods around them will look different. Restart your launcher and the pack will update itself.

In this update

  • Trees are now their real size. A forest oak comes to about 25 blocks and a spruce reaches 30 or more.
  • There are 35 kinds of tree. Each biome has its own mix, and two trees of the same kind will not grow into the same shape.
  • Trunks look more natural, big trees have roots at the base, old trees carry moss, and the leaves are fuller. The game also runs faster in the woods.
  • Trees keep growing slowly after they are planted, and if you look at one the tooltip tells you what kind it is.
  • Your builds, claims, rails, fruit trees and mangroves have not been touched. Land nobody had visited yet will come in with the new forests when you first get there.

Up close

What you notice up close, under the trees.

Trunks, roots and moss

Thick trunks have their corners cut back so they look more natural. Big trees have roots spreading out at the foot, and old trees have moss on their lowest blocks.

Layered crowns

The leaves grow in layers with gaps between them, so you can see the branches instead of one solid ball of leaves.

Young and old trees

A wood has young trees, grown trees and a few old giants in it. About one in twenty is a giant.

Names on every tree

If you look at a trunk, a leaf block or a sapling, the tooltip tells you which kind of tree it belongs to.

Behind the scenes

The short version of how it was done. It took about ten days and five test servers, and none of the trees were placed by hand.

1. Measure the world

A script went through all 1.84 million chunks and noted every tree, dead root, leaf block and biome, along with where players had been. In this picture, the brighter an area is, the more it has been visited.

2. Grow the trees in a garden

Six copies of each kind were planted on a flat test platform and left to grow, and every stage was recorded on the way up.

3. Change the tree mod

The tree mod, Dynamic Trees, limits how big a tree can get. New Haven now runs its own modified copy of it, which allows wider crowns and adds the shaped trunks, roots and moss.

4. Decide what grows where

Two lists were made for all 80 biomes, one based on what a forester would expect and one based on climate and geography, and then combined so that neighbouring biomes each have a tree you will not see next door.

5. Test by camera

Each attempt was planted on a copy of the world and photographed from the same 32 spots by a game client running on its own, which took under two minutes a round.

6. Convert the world

Land nobody had visited generates with the new forests when someone gets there. Land with builds or play time on it was replanted overnight on five servers, checked tree by tree, and swapped into the live world during a short stop. There was a backup before every step.
